## `/blog/electronic-waivers-for-trampoline-parks`

Live review found unverified injury statistics, broad enforceability assertions and a suggestion that electronic waivers solve every operational problem. Keep the URL and useful educational intent. Suggested title: “Electronic Waivers for Trampoline Parks: A Family Arrival Checklist”.

Replacement draft:

Electronic waivers can move reading and signing ahead of the reception queue. They do not replace attraction rules, a safety briefing, guardian-authority checks or the admission decision. Have qualified counsel review your wording and signing requirements for your jurisdiction and activities; an electronic signature alone does not establish that a liability waiver is enforceable.

### Before party day

Send the published waiver link in the organizer's arrival instructions. Forwarding a link provides access to the form, not consent on behalf of all invited children. Specify which responsible adult must complete the document under your venue's policy.

### At reception

Keep a QR code where families can pause and read. Offer an online kiosk tablet when someone needs a device. Configure and test minors and group signing where appropriate. Group signing can place several participants on one signed document; it does not verify that the signer has authority for every child.

### Before admission

Have staff retrieve the signed record, check the participant and guardian details and resolve exceptions before issuing admission items. A signature is not a booking match, a wristband or proof of staff approval. Maintain connectivity and a documented exception process.

### Keep the original record

When wording changes, publish a new version. Earlier signatures should retain the exact version signed and the stored PDF, rather than being regenerated from today's wording. Test retrieval and exports with synthetic details during the trial.

## First, the reassuring part: nothing you've collected is at risk

The fear that stops most people from switching is "I'll lose my signed waivers." You won't. Smartwaiver states that your data belongs to you and that you can export it whenever you want. Your already-signed waivers are historical legal records — you **archive** them, you don't migrate them into another live system. The new tool starts collecting *new* signatures; the old ones live on as exported files you keep.

So a migration is really just three moving parts: **get your data out**, **stand up the new waiver**, and **redirect the people who sign it.**

## Step 1 — Export everything out of Smartwaiver

Do this first, while your account is still active and on its current plan (export options can depend on your plan, so don't downgrade until you've pulled everything).

- **Signed PDFs.** Download your signed waivers as individual PDFs — these are the copies that matter if a signature is ever questioned. Grab the full set, not just a sample.
- **The response data as CSV.** Export the field-level data (names, dates, answers, timestamps) to a CSV so you have a searchable index of who signed and when.
- **Verify the audit-trail metadata came with it.** Open a few exported PDFs and confirm the signing timestamp, the signer details, and the waiver text are all present. That metadata *is* the evidentiary value — check it survived the export before you rely on it.
- **Store it where you'll actually find it.** A dated folder in your business drive, backed up. These are records you may need years later, long after the account is closed.

Once your data is safely exported and spot-checked, the rest is just setup.

## Step 2 — What transfers, and what you rebuild

Being honest about this up front prevents surprises. Waiver platforms don't have a "port my account" button — but the only thing you truly rebuild is the template, and that takes minutes.

| Item | Transfers automatically? | What you do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Historical signed waivers | No — they're archived, not moved | Export as PDF + CSV (Step 1), keep for retention |
| Your waiver's wording | No | Upload the PDF and let AI rebuild it as a signable form |
| Signing link / QR code | No — you get a new one | Reprint and replace at every entry point (Step 3) |
| Kiosk / tablet setup | No | Point the tablet at the new kiosk page |
| Integrations & webhooks | No | Reconnect your booking system / POS / CRM (Step 4) |

The template is the only "content" you recreate, and you don't retype it. With FlatWaiver you upload the PDF of your current Smartwaiver waiver (a photo or Word file works too) and AI converts it into a signable form, preserving every clause for your review. Publishing then version-locks that exact text to every future signature — so from your first new signer, the record pins the precise wording they agreed to.

## Step 3 — Redirect the people who sign

This is what actually makes the switch "live." Your customers reach your waiver through a handful of entry points — change each one to the new link:

1. **The front-desk QR code / poster** — reprint with the new URL.
2. **Booking and confirmation emails** — swap the waiver link so people who sign ahead land on the new form.
3. **Your website's "sign our waiver" button.**
4. **The walk-in kiosk tablet** — set its home page to the new kiosk URL.

A signing link is just a URL, so there's no technical conflict in having both old and new active at once — which sets up the safe part.

## Step 4 — Reconnect integrations, then run in parallel

If your reservation system, POS, or CRM was wired to Smartwaiver (webhooks, key-tag check-in, booking sync), reconnect those to the new platform. If your "integration" was really just a QR code and a front-desk tablet — which is the case for most operators — there's nothing to reconnect.

Then run both for **two to four weeks**:

- New signatures come in on the new tool; Smartwaiver stays live for anything in flight.
- Sign a **test waiver** end to end — confirm the signature captures, the confirmation email sends, the record stores, and the export format is what you need.
- Watch a full week of real traffic land correctly before you cancel.

Because FlatWaiver's trial is 14 days with no card required, you can complete this entire parallel run and see real signatures arrive before you pay for anything or cancel anything.

## Step 5 — Cancel (and keep your archive)

Once new signatures are flowing cleanly and you've confirmed your export is complete, cancel Smartwaiver. One caution for **seasonal businesses**: if you close the account, confirm you've exported everything first — retaining access to records inside Smartwaiver otherwise means staying on its storage plan. That's exactly the recurring cost a clean, self-held export removes.

## Why climbing gyms outgrow generic e-sign tools

A contract-signing tool may collect a signature, but check whether it also supports these front-desk needs:

- **Volume.** Day passes, intro classes and events can change your monthly signature count. Compare what your plan costs in a quiet month and a busy one — see our [waiver software pricing breakdown](/blog/waiver-software-cost).

- **The floor is the point.** Staff need to look up someone's signed waiver by name at the desk during a rush.

Use these needs to compare dedicated waiver tools as well as any signing features already included in your gym software.

Two of these deserve special attention for climbing specifically.

### Flagged answers help staff review responses

Configure an answer such as "first time climbing" to flag the signed record for staff review. The desk can then decide whether to discuss orientation or other next steps; the flag does not automatically route the visitor. FlatWaiver marks records when submitted answers match the waiver’s configured flag values. When the medical-condition question and its detail field are configured, a yes answer requires the detail field before submission. Staff still need to review the responses and follow the gym’s procedures.

### Keep the signed text and its version together

When reviewing a signed waiver, staff need the text presented at signing, the timestamp and the participant’s details. FlatWaiver keeps signatures tied to the published template version and stores the signed PDF with a hash for integrity checks. Later edits to a template do not replace the version attached to an earlier signature. ## Climbing gym waiver setup: a front-desk playbook

Once you've picked a tool, work through this setup checklist and test the signing flow before using it at reception:

1. **Convert your existing waiver.** Upload your PDF, photo or Word document. FlatWaiver creates a draft; compare the converted text and fields with the original, correct any issues, then publish the reviewed version. Conversion is a starting point, not a guarantee of transcription accuracy.
2. **Add the climbing-specific fields.** Experience level (dropdown), belay certification (checkbox), medical condition (yes/no with required detail on yes — flagged), emergency contact, date of birth, and the guardian flow for minors.
3. **Share the link and QR code.** Add your signing link to booking-confirmation emails and the party-booking page. Display a printed QR code at the desk so walk-ins can sign on their own phones. These are links you place yourself, not an automatic booking-system integration.
4. **Stand up a kiosk tablet** for walk-ins: open the kiosk link and use the tablet’s device settings if you want to restrict it to that page. The signing form resets automatically after each completed signature.
5. **Train the desk on two moves:** search a name to confirm a returning climber's waiver, and glance at flags before sending anyone to orientation. In FlatWaiver both live on one Front Desk screen with one-tap check-in.
6. **Do a monthly export** (CSV + PDFs) into your own storage. Whatever tool you use — your records should also live somewhere you control.

## Common questions from gym owners

**Do returning climbers sign every visit?** FlatWaiver lets staff look up an existing signed waiver. Your gym should decide when a new signature is needed based on its activities, waiver wording and advice for its jurisdiction; the software does not make that decision.

**What about parties and groups?** Send the signing link with the party confirmation email so parents can complete guardian details before arrival. Staff can look up individual signed records when the group checks in.

**Can we brand it?** You should. The waiver is often a guest's first interaction with your gym; your logo and colors on the signing page (and on the stored PDF) keep it from feeling like a third-party detour.

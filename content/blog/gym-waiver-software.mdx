## What gym waiver software actually needs to do

Any e-sign tool can collect a signature. A gym breaks the generic ones, because your volume and your front desk are relentless. Judge tools on these:

- **Phone-first signing your members finish before they arrive.** A link in the booking/confirmation email and a QR code at the door mean most people sign on their own phone in under a minute — no line at the desk.
- **A walk-in kiosk.** A tablet in a locked-down kiosk mode that resets after each signer, for the day-pass crowd who didn't sign ahead.
- **Fast front-desk lookup.** Search any member by name in seconds to confirm they've signed — during a rush, not after.
- **A real minor/guardian flow.** Kids' classes, youth programs, and birthday parties mean parents signing for children constantly. You need guardian name, relationship, and signature captured with the minor's details — not a bolted-on second signature box.
- **Flagged screening answers.** A "yes" to a medical-condition question should surface to your staff as a badge, not sit buried in a PDF.
- **Evidence-grade records.** Timestamp, IP, the exact waiver version signed, the consent statement — the things that matter the day a claim is questioned.
- **Pricing that doesn't scale with your foot traffic** (more on that below).

## Recurring members: sign once, look up forever

Members shouldn't re-sign every visit. Good gym waiver software stores the signature and lets you look the member up by name at the desk. Many gyms re-collect annually so the record stays current relative to any incident — that's a policy choice, not a legal requirement. Either way, you want search-by-name and a clear view of who has a waiver on file.

## Minors and guardian signing

If you run youth classes, kids' camps, or birthday parties, guardian signing is not an edge case — it's a big share of your volume. The flow should capture the participant's details, then the parent or guardian's full legal name, relationship, and a separate signature, all stored on one record. (Whether a parent can waive a child's *own* claims varies by state — we cover that in [are digital waivers legally binding?](/blog/are-digital-waivers-legally-binding).)

## Getting set up: an afternoon, not a project

You don't rebuild your waiver. The fastest path:

1. **Upload the gym waiver you already use** — a PDF, a photo, or a Word file — and let AI convert it into a signable form, preserving your wording for your review.
2. **Add your fields**: name, email, date of birth, emergency contact, a medical-condition question (flagged), and the guardian flow for minors.
3. **Publish**, which version-locks the text to every future signature.
4. **Put the QR code everywhere** — the door, the desk, booking-confirmation emails, the party-booking page — and **stand up a kiosk tablet** for walk-ins.
